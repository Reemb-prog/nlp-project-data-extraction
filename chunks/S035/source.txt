Business IT Management

Program Overview

Success in the workplace in the twenty-first century can hardly be achieved without
knowledge, skills, and experience in using modern technology and its components, such
as information and communication technology (ICT), computer-based systems, and
business applications.

The Business IT Management program combines business and information technology
to enable students to gain a thorough understanding of how IT and computer applications
improve effectiveness, increase efficiency, and facilitate business operations.

Students are thoroughly exposed to the business uses of database management,
programming, networking, telecommunications, e-commerce, the internet, and online
development.

To obtain a Bachelor of Business Administration degree in Business IT Management,
the student must complete 99 credit hours. These hours include University requirements
(UR), College requirements (CR), and Program requirements (PR). The allocation of the
credit hours is shown in the following table:

B.  Major Electives

As part of the Bachelor of Business Administration in Business IT Management program,
the student must study 3 credit hours of major electives. Major electives could be upper-
level courses in Accounting or closely related business or computing areas. These
courses allow students  to focus on a  specific area  for in-depth knowledge and
understanding. Meanwhile, they introduce  flexibility into the program, allowing the
integration of emerging topics.

C.  Entrepreneurship

The instructor leads the students through the detailed aspects of starting a business,
from identifying an opportunity and conducting a feasibility study to preparing a business
plan covering marketing, operations, and finance. Students also learn about funding,
expansion, and franchising strategies. Prerequisite: Senior Standing.

D.  Co-op Work Experience

Each student must complete 8 weeks of practical training in a field related to their
interests. The Co-op experience is usually fulfilled during the summer semester of the
third year in the program. The Co-op office matches the Co-op assignment with the
student’s field of study and the employer’s interest. A faculty member follows up on a
student’s progress throughout the Co-op period by conducting field visits and ensuring
that the student’s performance is aligned with their aspirations and the employer’s needs.
Students must then submit formal reports and posters and make formal presentations
about their Co-op experience. While ENGL 217 and Senior Standing are prerequisites
for co-op training, the program must stipulate additional conditions.

Career Opportunities

An increasing number of companies and entrepreneurial ventures in the Middle East and
North Africa (MENA) region have sought to capitalize on the computing and information
technology revolution to enable and facilitate business operations in areas such as
telemarketing, e-commerce, e-government, and social media. This creates numerous
job opportunities for people with skills in online sales, electronic market research, e-
commerce, online transaction security, mobile application development, and information
systems management.

Courses Description

I.  Mandatory Courses

Descriptions of the major mandatory courses are given below.

 BADM 485    Co-op Work Experience                                 1(0,1)
 The Co-op work experience is designed to provide students with full-time work terms
  in business, industry, or government. The main thrust of this course is the opportunity
  to put into practice the major aspects of the student’s business education. Special
 emphasis will be placed on assessing the student's attitude and work ethics. Students
  will be encouraged to network in the industry and to participate in professional
 organizations. Prerequisite: ENGL 217; Senior Standing.

 BADM 490    Entrepreneurship
 The instructor leads the students through the detailed aspects of starting a business,
 from identifying an opportunity and conducting a feasibility study to preparing a
 business plan covering marketing, operations, and finance. Students also learn about
 funding, expansion, and franchising strategies. Prerequisite: Senior Standing.

 BITM 305     Introduction to Programming                           3(2,2)
 This is a foundation course for  all computer programming courses.  It enhances
 programming  skills and presents the fundamentals  of structured programming
 concepts  in C. The course covers  primitive data  types, expressions,  control
 statements, functions, and arrays.  It also provides a hands-on experience with
 MATLAB. Prerequisite: BITM 200 (equivalent to COSC 214).

 BITM 310     Database Management Systems                        3(3,1)
 Students will explore advanced database concepts, including automation techniques,
 using popular Windows-based DBMS software. The course includes the following
  topics: the planning, creation, and maintenance of databases; the development of
 information retrieval techniques; the design and customization of forms; and the
 creation and use of macros and modules in an automated system. Prerequisite:
 BITM 305; equivalent to COSC 231 and CCEE 315.

 BITM 340    The Development Tools of Information Systems         3(3,0)
 This course offers a traditional look at the system's life cycle process. Modeling and
 charting will be included. The tools and methodology applied by the systems analyst
  will be studied. The course covers an overview of the system  life cycle, UML
 Diagrams, feasibility analysis, project management, HCI, and the golden rules of user
 interface design. Prerequisite: BITM 305; equivalent to COSC 341 and CCEE 510.

 BITM 355    Networking                                               3(2,2)
 An  introduction  to data communications and networking, covering networking
 hardware/operating system concepts, modems, WAN, and LAN standards and
 protocols. Prerequisite: Junior Standing; Equivalent to COSC 360, CCEE 354.

BITM 401   Web Programming                                        3(3,1)
 This course teaches students how to develop and implement web-based programs,
 with an emphasis on  interface programming.   It  introduces  students  to web
 development,  client-side languages, and the styles needed to build functional,
 responsive websites. The course covers HTML5, CSS3, JavaScript/jQuery, and
 responsive design. Equivalent to COSC 333, CCEE 411.

 BITM 415     Business Intelligence                                   3(2,1)
 This course introduces business intelligence as computerized support for managerial
 decision-making. It focuses on the theoretical and conceptual foundations of business
  intelligence, as well as on commercial tools and techniques for effective decision
 support.  It focuses on extracting business intelligence from data sets for various
 applications, such as reporting and visual analytics across domains like web and
 business analytics, to aid decision-making. Provides hands-on experience with a
 variety of business intelligence software for reporting and building visualizations and
 dashboards. Prerequisite: Senior Standing.

 BITM 450    Big Data Analytics                                        3(2,1)
 This course provides students with a comprehensive understanding of big data
 analytics from a Business Information Technology (BIT) perspective, with a strong
 emphasis on Apache Hadoop and  its ecosystem. The course focuses on how
 organizations leverage Hadoop-based platforms to store, process, analyze, and
 derive value from large-scale business data. Students will gain hands-on experience
 with HDFS, MapReduce, Hive, HBase, Pig, and ZooKeeper, and learn how these
 technologies support business intelligence, operational analytics, and managerial
 decision-making. The course prioritizes practical Hadoop applications, business use
 cases, and the interpretation of analytical outputs over advanced algorithmic theory.
 Through lectures, laboratory exercises, assignments, and a group project, students
  will design and implement end-to-end Hadoop-based analytics solutions to address
 real-world business problems. Prerequisite: BITM 350

 BMGT 300   Project Management                                     3(3,0)
 This course provides students with the skills to manage their business projects by
  effectively leading, organizing, scheduling, and controlling tasks to achieve project
 goals. Topics include project selection and statement of work, project manager skills,
 task breakdown structure, PERT/CPM scheduling, and budgeting. Prerequisite:
 Junior Standing.

BITM 320     Enterprise Resource Planning
Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP) systems are a major investment for businesses.
These systems are essential for maintaining competitiveness, meeting customer
demands, and improving efficiency and flexibility in a global marketplace. By adopting
ERP, companies can streamline their operations, align with industry best practices,
and fully utilize integrated data resources. This course will provide students with the
skills and knowledge needed to plan, design, and implement ERP systems effectively.
Prerequisite: BITM 300

BITM 402   Advanced Web Programming                            3(3,1)
This course focuses on server-side programming. It allows students to learn how to
connect their website or web application to a database and to save and retrieve data
from it. The course exposes students to web controls, validation controls, data source
controls, data binding controls, state management, and working with third-party media
like XML and web services. Prerequisite: BITM 401; equivalent to COSC 434 and
CCEE 514.

BITM 410   Advanced Programming and Data Structures           3(3,1)
This is a continuation course using advanced C++.  The student studies object-
oriented programming concepts such as classes, objects, polymorphism, data hiding,
and encapsulation. This course gives the student a new perspective on how to think
about objects. Prerequisite: BITM 305; equivalent to COSC 215 and CCEE 216.

BITM 455   Advanced Networking                                    3(2,2)
This course prepares students to act as System and Network Administrators by
implementing Active Directory Domain Services ADDS in distributed environments
that can include complex network services and domain controllers. The covered
materials help students efficiently automate the administration of users, groups, and
computers. Prerequisite: BITM 355, Equivalent to COSC 461.
