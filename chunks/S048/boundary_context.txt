ELEC 331L    Electric Machines Lab                                   1(0,2)
 This is a Lab course with experiments on electric machines. It covers the following
 topics: the basics of DC motors and generators; DC series, shunt, and separately
 excited motors and generators; single-phase AC induction machines; and 3-phase
 machines. Co-requisite: ELEC 331.

ELEC 340    Engineering Electromagnetics                          3(3,0)
 This course covers vector analysis, static electric fields, static magnetic fields, time-
 varying fields, Maxwell’s equations, and electromagnetic waves. Prerequisite: PHYS
 211.

ELEC 400   Summative Learning (BS) Project                       3(3,0)
Team-oriented, project-based experience culminating in the creation of an artifact;
milestones include project selection and proposal, a creative solution, a report, a
presentation, and a demonstration of the device. Prerequisite: ENGL 217 taken in
the last semester of the BS Program. Annually.

ELEC 405    Electrical System Design                                 3(3,0)
This course aims to teach students how to design various electrical systems, including
equipment selection, protection systems, EMC and safety requirements, international
standards,  drawing  and  interpreting  schematics  and  wiring  diagrams,  cost
considerations, and energy efficiency. Prerequisite: ELEC 210.

ELEC 406   Modeling and Simulation                                 3(3,0)
This course aims to teach students how to represent, characterize, understand,
analyze, and solve real-world problems through modeling and simulation. In this
course, students are introduced to the tools and techniques for modeling and simulating
systems, ranging from basic circuits to more complex technical systems across various
engineering domains. Prerequisite: PHYS 211 and ELEC 210.

ELEC 431    Introduction to Renewable Energy Systems             3(3,0)
 This course aims to provide students with information on renewable energy sources,
 including solar, wind, and wave energy, as well as biomass energy. This course will
 also illustrate how to link these sources to the grid and synchronize them to deliver
 electricity reliably and efficiently. Prerequisite: ELEC 210.

ELEC 432   Power Electronics                                        3(3,0)
 This course  familiarizes the students with basic power switch technology and
 associated electronic circuits. In this course, power electronic circuits and switching
 devices are studied. Their applications in AC/DC, DC/DC, DC/AC, and AC/AC
 converters, as well as switching power supplies, are studied. Prerequisite: ELEC
 320.

MINOR IN RENEWABLE ENERGY SYSTEMS

Rationale

The College of Engineering/ECE Department offers a minor in Renewable Energy
Systems (RES). The minor aims to address the expected national, regional, and
international need for personal experts in renewable energy systems for the generation,
transmission, and use of energy.

Program Objectives

The aims of the minor are to:

 •  Provide students with advanced expertise in renewable energy sources, systems,
    and policies;
 •  Provide students  with the necessary  tools  to  contribute  to the  fast-growing
    renewable energy systems sector;
 •  Prepare undergraduate students for graduate studies in renewable energy.

Learning Outcomes

At the end of this minor, the student is expected to demonstrate the ability to:
 •  Use current techniques, architectures, and tools to generate, store, and distribute
    energy from renewable sources;
 •  Design systems and tools to generate, store, and distribute renewable energy.

Program Requirements

While most  relevant  to  electrical engineering  students,  this minor  is  offered  to
undergraduate RHU students in the BE or MS program, from different engineering
disciplines.  It may also be offered to students with a BS/BE from other universities,
subject to a case-by-case evaluation of  their transcripts and other specific RHU
requirements.

Early  in  their major, interested RHU engineering students need to complete the
appropriate form declaring their intent to minor in RES while completing their regular
major.

To complete the RES minor, a student must
1)  Declare a minor in Renewable Energy Systems by completing the Minor Declaration
    Form;
2)  Obtain the approval of the dean of the student’s college major and the dean of the
    College of Engineering
3)  Earn a Minor Cumulative Grade Point Average of no less than 70%.
4)  Complete 18 credits of coursework as specified below.
