Minor in Computer Networks

Rationale

In today’s world, we cannot imagine a company without computer networks. Therefore,
networking skills are becoming a valuable asset for employees across domains.

In response to national, regional, and international needs and demand for experts in
computer networks, the College of Engineering/ECE Department and the College of Arts
and Sciences/CS Department offer a minor in Computer Networking.

Program’s Purpose

The main aim of the minor is to expose students from different backgrounds to the
concepts of computer networks and the protocols that allow devices and systems to
communicate.  It will equip them with the knowledge and skills needed to install and
operate networks at various scales. Furthermore, the minor will enable students to earn
the Cisco CCNA Routing and Switching certification. The minor shall also serve as good
preparation for further and more advanced graduate and/or professional studies in
computer networks.

Learning Outcomes

At the end of this minor, the student is expected to demonstrate the ability to:
•   Compare different computer network protocols;
•   Design simple computer networks;
•   Perform simple computer network troubleshooting;
•    Investigate simple computer network attacks.

Minor Requirements

This minor is structured to accommodate undergraduate RHU students in the BE or BS
program from different disciplines (engineering, computer science, or business). It may
also be offered to students with a BS/BE from other universities, subject to a case-by-
case evaluation of their transcripts and other specific RHU requirements.

Early in their studies, interested RHU students need to declare their intent to pursue a
minor in computer networks by completing the relevant minor declaration form and
informing their advisor.

To complete the computer networking minor, a student must

1.  Declare a minor in computer networks by completing the Minor Declaration Form;
2.  Obtain the approval of the Dean of the student’s college major and the Dean of the
    College of Engineering;

3.  Earn a Minor Cumulative Grade Point Average (MCGPA) of no less than 70%;
4.  Complete 19 credits of coursework as specified below.

It should be noted that common courses between the student's major requirements and
the computer networks minor requirements count toward both the major and the minor.
The student must complete a minimum of 3 credit hours of coursework that is not counted
toward the requirement for their major or any other minor.

Career Options

Computer network experts are well-suited to work in a variety of sectors, including but
not limited to telecommunications, hospitals, banks, the police, and the army.

Curriculum and Program

The computer networking minor consists of 5 courses and 4 labs (19 credits), of which 7
credits are mandatory, and the remaining credits are elective, selected to satisfy the
requirements of the proposed program objectives and learning outcomes.

Courses Description

CCEE 411  Web Programming                                         3(3,0)
 This course teaches students how to develop and implement web-based programs with
 an emphasis on interface programming. It introduces students to web development and
 the client-side and server-side languages and technologies needed to build functional,
 responsive websites. The course covers HTML5, CSS3, JavaScript/jQuery, PHP, and
 responsive design. Co-requisite: CCEE 214. Equivalent to COSC 333.

CCEE 354   Computer Networks                                      3(3,0)
 This course enables students to gain fundamental knowledge of computer networks,
 appreciate the trade-offs and choices in networking, learn to design and analyze
 protocols, and prepare to study advanced topics in the field. Co-requisite: CCEE 221.
 Equivalent to COSC 360.

CCEE 354L  Computer Networks Lab                                  1(0,2)
 This Lab helps prepare students seeking to pass advanced Cisco Certifications. The
 student will acquire knowledge of network equipment and protocol functionality, learn
 to build a simple Ethernet network using routers, switches, and computers, and use
 router CLI commands to perform basic configuration and verification. The student will
 also learn valuable network problem-solving techniques and concepts. Accompanying
 Lab for CCEE 354. Co-requisite: CCEE 354.

CCEE 514 Advanced Web Programming                              3(3,0)
 This course focuses on server-side programming. It teaches students how to connect
 their website or web application to a database and save and retrieve data from it. The
 course exposes students to web controls, validation controls, data source controls, data
 binding controls, state management, and working with third-party media such as XML
and web services. Prerequisite: CCEE 411/COSC 333. Equivalent to COSC 434.

CCEE 547  Optical Communications                                   3(3,0)
 This course is designed to progress from the description of the components in a fiber
 link to the interconnections within a link or a network. The components of fiber links will
be discussed (fiber, connections, sources, and receivers). These optical components
 will be integrated into a complete optical link. Prerequisite: CCEE 331.

 CCEE 552   Network Security                                        3(3,0)
 Network security is the protection of the underlying networking infrastructure from
 unauthorized access, misuse, or theft. It involves creating a secure infrastructure for
 devices, applications, and users to operate securely. Pre-requisite: CCEE 354 or
 COSC 360.

CCEE 553   Advanced Communication Networks                    3(3,0)
In this course, we will explore current network architectures and topologies, such as
telephone systems (PDH/SDH, SONET), Frame Relay, ATM, and MPLS, as well as
technologies  under  development. We  will  also  learn how  to assess  network
performance and which technologies are used to enhance service quality. At the end
of the course, the student will be able to identify the technologies to be used in a given
environment and study its performance. Prerequisite: CCEE 354.

CCEE 554L  Computer Networks Lab 2                               1(0,2)
This Lab helps prepare students seeking to pass advanced Cisco Certifications (CCNA
1). The student will acquire knowledge of network equipment and protocol functionality,
learn to build a simple Ethernet network using routers, switches, and computers, and
use router CLI commands to perform basic configuration and verification. The student
will also learn valuable network problem-solving techniques and concepts through
hands-on skills using hardware devices. Co-requisite: CCEE 354L.

CCEE 555   Advanced Computer Networks                          1(0,2)
This  is an advanced computer networks  course  that  requires  active  student
participation. In this course, we will explore TCP; Internet inter-domain routing; Internet
quality of service and traffic engineering; Internet traffic measurement and analysis;
data centers; the analysis and performance of content distribution networks; and
Software-defined  networking.  In  addition,  the  students  will  learn how  to  build
client/server computer network applications using sockets. Prerequisite: CCEE 354.

CCEE 555L  Computer Networks Lab 3                               1(0,2)
 This Lab helps prepare students seeking to pass the Cisco Routing and Switching
 Essentials (CCNA 2) Certification. The primary focus of this Lab is routers and switches
 in small networks. The student will learn the architecture, components, and operations
 of routers and switches, as well as their basic configuration and functionality. They will
 also learn how to configure and troubleshoot static and dynamic routing protocols,
 access-control lists, VLANs, DHCP for IPv4 and IPv6 networks, and NAT operations.
 Prerequisite: CCEE 354L.

CCEE 556   Network and System Administration                    3(3,0)
This course will help students become system administrators and install, configure,
maintain, and troubleshoot a Microsoft Windows Server Operating system. It provides
them with the knowledge and skills to work with Active Directory technologies in
environments including complex network services. This course also provides students
with basic knowledge of  installing, configuring, and maintaining Linux operating
systems. By the end of this course, students will have all the required materials to
submit and pass the MCSA: Windows Server certification exam. Prerequisite: CCEE
354.

CCEE 556L  Computer Networks Lab 4                                1(0,2)
This Lab helps prepare students seeking to pass the Cisco Routing and Switching
Essentials (CCNA 3) Certification. The primary focus of this Lab is routers and switches
in large and complex networks. The student will learn how to configure routers and
switches with advanced functionalities. They will also learn how to configure and
troubleshoot enhanced switching technologies, first-hop redundancy protocols in a
switched network, wireless routers and clients, and routers in complex networks.
Finally, they will learn how to manage CISCO IOS software licensing and configuration
 files. Prerequisite: CCEE 555L.
