MINOR   IN  INDUSTRIAL  ENGINEERING  AND
MANAGEMENT SYSTEMS

Rationale

The Industrial Engineering and Management Systems (IEMS) minor aims to expand
engineering students’ expertise in system-wide optimization, decision science, and
digital integration.  It prepares students to architect the "intelligence layer" of the
enterprise and prepares undergraduate students for graduate studies  in Industrial
Engineering, Engineering Management, and Operations Research.

It primarily targets mechanical and mechatronics engineering students, but  it also
accommodates  students  from  various  engineering  backgrounds,  provided   all
prerequisite courses are completed. The program focuses on mathematical optimization,
financial engineering, facility logistics, quality assurance, and digital twin simulation. By
the end of this minor, a student is expected to be proficient in designing, simulating, and
managing complex  industrial ecosystems  that are  cost-effective, data-driven, and
industry-ready.

Program participants are required to complete at least 18 credits of related coursework.
Although not required, with proper and timely advising, they may also complete their Co-
op work  experience  or  their BE  project  in  Industrial  Engineering  or Systems
Management, thereby enhancing their capabilities.

Program Objectives

The primary educational objectives of the IEMS minor program are to:

    •   Give engineering students a sophisticated understanding of Industrial
        Engineering principles and management systems.

    •   Equip students with the knowledge to pursue graduate studies in Industrial
         Engineering, Engineering Management, Systems Engineering, Data Analytics,
       and other related disciplines.

Learning Outcomes

After completing the minor program, students should have the ability to:

    •   Apply advanced mathematical modeling and operations research to optimize
        resource allocation and industrial scheduling.
    •   Analyze the financial viability of engineering projects using rigorous economic
         tools and ROI assessments.

Career Options

This minor allows IEMS holders to start careers in engineering management, systems
analytics, supply chain  architecture,  industrial  consultancy,  project management,
operations strategy, and digital manufacturing integration in both regional and global
markets.

Program Requirements

While most relevant to mechanical and mechatronics engineering students, this minor is
offered to undergraduate RHU students in the BS/BE program from different disciplines.
It may also be offered to students with a BS/BE from other universities, subject to a case-
by-case evaluation of  their  transcripts. Proper advising  is pertinent to the timely
completion of the minor. To complete the IEMS minor, a student must:

     1.  Declare an IEMS minor by completing the Minor Declaration Form;
     2.  Obtain the approval of the dean of the college major;
     3.  Obtain a Minor Cumulative Grade Point Average of no less than 70%;
     4.  Complete 18 credits of coursework as specified below.

IEMS Curriculum

The program offers a rich blend of mathematical optimization and  virtual system
modeling, equipping students with the tools to navigate the modern industrial world.
Accordingly, this minor shall include:

    •   Engineering financial management and project economics.
    •   Advanced quality engineering and lean operations.
    •   Mathematical optimization and deterministic operations research.
    •   Physical facility planning and systematic layout design.
    •    Digital Twin architecture and virtual system simulation.
    •   Global supply chain engineering and logistics optimization.

Mandatory Courses

The student must complete a minimum of 6 credit hours of coursework exclusive to the
IEMS minor that will not count toward requirements for their major or any other minor. A
description of the 18 credits from which a student must select is given below:

ENGR 300     Engineering Economics and Management             3(3,0)
 Concepts and techniques in basic Engineering economy principles and applications.
 Interest and financial mathematics: present worth, annual worth, benefit/cost ratio,
 internal rate of return, multiple alternatives, income tax,  inflation, Risk analysis,
 Investment and investment choice, equivalence, loans, cost of capital, retirement and
 replacement, sector analysis and viewpoint, sensitivity analysis, accounting, and
 financial statements.

 BMGT 410    Quality Management                                    3(3,0)
 This course highlights that TQM has become crucial for business excellence in
 worldwide markets. It allows students to learn techniques that establish sustainable
  quality improvement in both product and service industries. Topics include seven
  quality control tools; SPC; quality function deployment and the house of quality;
 product reliability; Six Sigma; Benchmarking; quality certification and awards; and
  quality costing. Prerequisite: BADM 250

 ENGR 400    Operations Research                                    3(3,0)
 This course introduces mathematical modeling and  algorithmic techniques  for
 optimizing decision-making in complex engineering systems. Students will master the
 application of Linear Programming and the Simplex Method to solve problems
 involving constrained resources. Key topics include duality and sensitivity analysis
 (determining how changes in the system affect the optimal solution), network flow
 optimization (shortest-path and PERT/CPM), and integer programming. The course
 emphasizes  translating  real-world  industrial  challenges—such  as  production
 scheduling, trajectory planning for robots, and resource allocation—into rigorous
 mathematical frameworks. Prerequisite: MATH 311

 ENGR 410     Supply Chain Engineering & Logistics                3(3,0)
 This course applies engineering and mathematical principles to the design and
 optimization of global supply chain networks. Moving beyond general procurement,
 students  will focus on the quantitative modeling of inventory control (including
 deterministic and stochastic models), warehousing operations, and transportation
  logistics. Key topics include the "Bullwhip Effect" in multi-echelon systems, facility
 location modeling using center-of-gravity methods, and the strategic integration of
 Just-in-Time (JIT) principles. The course specifically addresses Design for Logistics
 (DFL), teaching students how mechanical design decisions (packaging, weight, and
 modularity) impact the total landed cost and supply chain resilience in a global market.
 Prerequisite: MATH 351 and ENGR 300

MECH 424     Manufacturing Systems & Facilities Planning         3(3,0)
 This course  focuses on  the  strategic  design and  organization  of  integrated
 manufacturing environments. Moving from individual machine operations to system-
 wide flow, students will study Systematic Layout Planning (SLP), group technology,
 and  the  design  of  Cellular  Manufacturing  units. The  curriculum emphasizes
 quantitative methods  for  line balancing, machine-requirement  calculations, and
 material-handling systems. Students will analyze the physical constraints of the shop
  floor to minimize "waste" in movement and optimize the integration of automated
 workstations and robotic cells. Prerequisite: MECH 224 and MECH 422

 MECA 425      Digital Twin & System Simulation                     3(3,0)
 This course explores the creation and use of Digital Twins to model, simulate, and
 predict the behavior of complex manufacturing and mechatronic systems. Utilizing
 discrete-event simulation (DES) software, students will develop virtual replicas of
 physical systems to analyze the impact of stochastic variables, such as machine
 downtimes and fluctuating arrival rates. The course covers data distribution fitting,
 model verification, and the statistical validation of simulation outputs. By performing
  "what-if" experimental analyses, students will learn to identify system bottlenecks and
 optimize throughput in a risk-free virtual environment before physical deployment.
 Prerequisite: MATH 351 and MECA 541
