MECH 522   Mechanism Synthesis: Classical Kinematics to        3(3,0)
             AI-Driven Optimization
This course  introduces  the  theory and computational  practice  of mechanism
synthesis, with emphasis on the design of four-bar linkages for path, motion, and
function generation. Classical kinematic synthesis methods are integrated with
numerical optimization techniques, including Newton–Raphson, least-squares, and
gradient-based methods, as well as intelligent metaheuristic algorithms such as
genetic algorithms, tabu search, and ant colony optimization. The course also exposes
students to data-driven and AI-assisted approaches, including deep neural networks
for inverse mechanism design, as advanced computational tools for automation,
design-space  exploration,  and  decision  support   in  mechanism  synthesis.
Computational projects provide students with hands-on experience in mechanism
modeling, simulation, error analysis, optimization, and the synthesis of mechanical
systems under practical design constraints. Prerequisite: MECH 321.

MINOR   IN  INDUSTRIAL  ENGINEERING  AND
MANAGEMENT SYSTEMS

Rationale

The Industrial Engineering and Management Systems (IEMS) minor aims to expand
engineering students’ expertise in system-wide optimization, decision science, and
digital integration.  It prepares students to architect the "intelligence layer" of the
enterprise and prepares undergraduate students for graduate studies  in Industrial
Engineering, Engineering Management, and Operations Research.

It primarily targets mechanical and mechatronics engineering students, but  it also
accommodates  students  from  various  engineering  backgrounds,  provided   all
prerequisite courses are completed. The program focuses on mathematical optimization,
financial engineering, facility logistics, quality assurance, and digital twin simulation. By
the end of this minor, a student is expected to be proficient in designing, simulating, and
managing complex  industrial ecosystems  that are  cost-effective, data-driven, and
industry-ready.

Program participants are required to complete at least 18 credits of related coursework.
Although not required, with proper and timely advising, they may also complete their Co-
op work  experience  or  their BE  project  in  Industrial  Engineering  or Systems
Management, thereby enhancing their capabilities.

Program Objectives

The primary educational objectives of the IEMS minor program are to:

    •   Give engineering students a sophisticated understanding of Industrial
        Engineering principles and management systems.

    •   Equip students with the knowledge to pursue graduate studies in Industrial
         Engineering, Engineering Management, Systems Engineering, Data Analytics,
       and other related disciplines.

Learning Outcomes

After completing the minor program, students should have the ability to:

    •   Apply advanced mathematical modeling and operations research to optimize
        resource allocation and industrial scheduling.
    •   Analyze the financial viability of engineering projects using rigorous economic
         tools and ROI assessments.
